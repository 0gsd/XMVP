# The Margin

**Title:** The Margin  
**Author:** Clods-Play Ensemble  
**Draft date:** 2026-01-27  
**Characters:** Martin, Vera, Claire, Hugh

---

INT. CONSERVATORY - AFTERNOON

*The room holds the golden dust of late summer light. MARTIN (55) stands near a dying rubber plant on a pedestal, watering can tilted. Water pools in the saucer beneath. He keeps pouring. VERA (52) sits in a wicker chair, a magazine open in her lap that she isn't reading. The pages haven't turned in some time.*

MARTIN

There.

*Pause.*

That should do.

VERA

Yes.

MARTIN

They need consistency.

VERA

Of course.

MARTIN

People underwater them. Or they forget entirely. Both extremes.

VERA

You've always been good with—

MARTIN

It's not about being good. It's about paying attention.

*He sets down the can with precision, exactly where it was before. The plant's leaves are brown at the edges, curling.*

VERA

I thought we might have the chicken.

MARTIN

The chicken.

VERA

For dinner. The one I defrosted. Unless you'd rather—

MARTIN

When did you defrost it?

VERA

This morning. Or last night. I can't—last night, I think.

MARTIN

You think.

VERA

Last night.

*Pause.*

Unless that's too long?

MARTIN

No, that's—that's within the window.

VERA

Good. Good, I thought it was, but you know I sometimes—

MARTIN

It's fine.

VERA

Right.

*Silence.*

With the potatoes, maybe. The small ones.

MARTIN

The fingerlings.

VERA

Yes. Those.

MARTIN

They take longer.

VERA

I know.

MARTIN

So you'd need to start them earlier than you think.

VERA

I will.

MARTIN

People always underestimate root vegetables.

VERA

I'll start them early.

*The fan completes another rotation. MARTIN examines the plant more closely, touching a brown leaf. It comes away in his fingers. He looks at it for a moment, then slips it into his pocket.*

VERA

She hasn't come down.

*Pause.*

Since this morning.

MARTIN

Who?

VERA

Claire.

MARTIN

Ah.

VERA

Since breakfast. Or before breakfast. I brought her tea around nine, and she was—well, she thanked me. She was very polite.

MARTIN

That's good.

VERA

But she hasn't—

MARTIN

Perhaps she's resting.

VERA

Yes.

MARTIN

It's a long journey.

VERA

Not that long.

MARTIN

Long enough.

*Pause.*

People need time to adjust.

VERA

I just thought she might—I don't know. Come down. See the garden. It's lovely in this light.

MARTIN

You could suggest it.

VERA

Oh, I wouldn't want to—

MARTIN

Why not?

VERA

Intrude.

MARTIN

It's your house.

*VERA's laugh comes too quickly, too high.*

VERA

Our house.

MARTIN

Yes.

VERA

And hers now. While she's—for however long she—

MARTIN

The guest room.

VERA

What?

MARTIN

You said 'her room.' It's the guest room.

VERA

I meant—yes. Of course. The guest room.

*Long pause.*

How long do you think—

MARTIN

Did she say?

VERA

Not exactly.

MARTIN

Then we can't know.

VERA

No.

MARTIN

It would be presumptuous to ask.

VERA

I suppose.

MARTIN

When my sister visited—you remember—I gave her a week. Precisely. I told her at the outset: "Seven days, Margaret. That's the shape of hospitality."

*Pause.*

She understood perfectly. Left on the sixth day, actually. Said she'd seen enough.

VERA

This is different.

MARTIN

Is it?

VERA

Claire isn't your sister.

MARTIN

No. She's yours.

*He looks at the plant again.*

Which makes the question of duration even more delicate.

VERA

You're right.

*Pause.*

I shouldn't wonder about things.

*She touches her temple.*

I used to be better at not—

*Stops herself, laughs wrongly.*

Sorry. Ignore me.

MARTIN

These rings.

VERA

What? Oh. Yes. Those are from—I put the vase there yesterday. The condensation. I meant to wipe them. I'm sorry.

MARTIN

Water rings.

VERA

I know. I should have been more careful.

MARTIN

They're permanent now.

VERA

Yes.

*Pause.*

I could get placemats. Nice ones. To cover—

MARTIN

That's not the point.

VERA

No. Of course not.

*She closes the magazine. Opens it again to a different page.*

Did you want lemon with it?

MARTIN

With what?

VERA

The chicken.

MARTIN

I hadn't thought about lemon.

VERA

You usually like lemon.

MARTIN

Do I?

VERA

With chicken. And fish, obviously. And sometimes the pork, though not always.

MARTIN

I don't think I'm particular about lemon.

VERA

Maybe I'm thinking of someone else.

MARTIN

Who else would you be thinking of?

VERA

No one. I meant—just in general. People like lemon.

MARTIN

Some people.

VERA

Yes.

*Silence.*

I'll get some anyway. Just in case.

*MARTIN returns to the plant, tilts his head at it.*

MARTIN

Did you move my papers?

*VERA goes very still.*

VERA

Which papers?

MARTIN

From the desk.

VERA

The one in the hall?

MARTIN

Yes.

VERA

I needed the space.

MARTIN

For what?

VERA

Her things. Claire's things. She had a bag, and I thought—

MARTIN

There are other surfaces.

VERA

I know. I should have asked. I'm sorry.

MARTIN

I had them arranged.

VERA

I was careful. I stacked them—

MARTIN

I had them arranged.

*Pause.*

In a particular order.

VERA

I didn't realize.

MARTIN

No.

VERA

Where should I—would you like me to put them back?

MARTIN

It doesn't matter now.

VERA

Martin—

MARTIN

The order is lost.

*He says this as if describing a death.*

VERA

I could try to remember—

MARTIN

Don't.

*VERA folds her hands in her lap, then unfolds them, then folds them again.*

VERA

I was only trying to make space.

MARTIN

I didn't ask you to make space.

VERA

No.

MARTIN

So why did you?

VERA

I thought—

*Pause.*

She's our guest.

MARTIN

You keep saying that.

VERA

What?

MARTIN

Our guest. She's family.

VERA

Yes.

MARTIN

So you don't need to—

VERA

What?

MARTIN

Perform.

*VERA's face does something complicated.*

VERA

I'm not performing.

MARTIN

Good.

VERA

I'm just trying to—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Make her comfortable.

MARTIN

By rearranging everything?

VERA

Not everything. Just the—it was only the desk, Martin.

MARTIN

And the room.

VERA

What about the room?

MARTIN

You changed the sheets.

VERA

Of course I changed the sheets.

MARTIN

They were clean.

VERA

They'd been on for—

MARTIN

A week. Two at most.

VERA

Three.

MARTIN

That's acceptable.

VERA

For a guest?

MARTIN

She's not a—

*He stops.*

*Long pause. The fan rotates. Once. Twice. The sound of something growing in the garden outside—or dying. Hard to tell which.*

VERA

Would you like a drink?

MARTIN

It's three o'clock.

VERA

Is it?

MARTIN

Nearly.

VERA

I lose track in here. The light's always—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

The same. Sort of. Golden.

*Pause.*

Preserved.

MARTIN

That doesn't make sense.

VERA

No.

*She touches the magazine, not opening it.*

I could make coffee.

MARTIN

You've already had two cups.

VERA

Have I?

MARTIN

This morning. I heard the machine.

VERA

You're right. Yes. Two cups.

*Pause.*

Maybe tea then.

MARTIN

I don't want tea.

VERA

No. Of course.

*Long pause.*

She might want tea.

MARTIN

Then ask her.

VERA

I brought her some. Earlier.

MARTIN

So she's had tea.

VERA

The cup was still full when I—

MARTIN

When you what?

VERA

Nothing. I shouldn't have—

MARTIN

Checked?

VERA

I wasn't checking. I was passing by, and the door was—

MARTIN

Open?

VERA

Slightly. Just slightly.

MARTIN

And?

VERA

The tea was there. On the bedside table. Cold probably.

*Pause.*

She was facing the wall.

MARTIN

Sleeping?

VERA

I don't know.

*MARTIN touches the plant again, differently this time. A diagnosis.*

MARTIN

Did she seem—

VERA

What?

MARTIN

Distressed.

VERA

I couldn't see her face.

MARTIN

But her posture.

VERA

Still. Very still.

MARTIN

People are still when they're resting.

VERA

Yes.

*Silence.*

But there's still, and then there's—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Absent.

*MARTIN turns to look at her properly for the first time.*

MARTIN

Absent.

VERA

That's not—I don't mean she wasn't there. Obviously she was there. I saw her.

MARTIN

Then what do you mean?

VERA

Elsewhere.

MARTIN

That's the same thing.

VERA

No.

*Pause.*

It's different.

*MARTIN returns his attention to the plant. He touches a leaf, examines the soil, runs his finger along the pot's rim.*

MARTIN

This needs repotting.

VERA

Does it?

MARTIN

The roots are probably bound. Circling. Strangling themselves, essentially.

*Pause.*

I've been watering it consistently. Every day. Same time. Same amount. That's what they require—predictability. Order.

VERA

Martin—

MARTIN

Most people give up too soon. They see a few brown leaves and they assume failure. But if you maintain the routine, if you don't deviate—

VERA

The plant is dead.

MARTIN

There's still green at the base.

VERA

That's the pot. The glaze.

MARTIN

No. Look here. This stem.

VERA

Brown.

MARTIN

With green underneath. You have to know where to look.

*He continues examining it, absorbed.*

Three weeks I've been giving it exactly what it needs. Morning light. Consistent moisture. I even researched the optimal temperature range—did you know they prefer sixty-five to seventy-five degrees? I've been monitoring.

*Pause.*

That's commitment. That's care.

*He looks up, realizes VERA hasn't spoken. She's watching him with something that might be pity.*

What?

VERA

Nothing.

MARTIN

You're looking at me.

VERA

I'm listening.

*Silence. MARTIN returns to the plant, more carefully now.*

MARTIN

People think gardening is about instinct. It's not. It's about method. Discipline. If you follow the proper steps—

VERA

It's dead, Martin.

MARTIN

You're not qualified to make that determination.

VERA

I can see it.

MARTIN

Seeing isn't knowing.

*Pause.*

I've done everything correctly.

VERA

I know.

MARTIN

Then how can it be—

*He stops.*

VERA

Sometimes things die anyway.

*MARTIN picks up the watering can again.*

MARTIN

It needs consistency.

VERA

It needs to be thrown away.

MARTIN

That's wasteful.

VERA

Keeping it is wasteful.

MARTIN

Why?

VERA

Your time. The water. The—

*Pause.*

The pretending.

*MARTIN sets down the can. Doesn't look at her.*

MARTIN

I'm not pretending.

VERA

No.

MARTIN

I'm being thorough.

VERA

Yes.

*Long pause.*

What if she doesn't come down?

MARTIN

She will.

VERA

But what if she doesn't?

MARTIN

Then she doesn't.

VERA

For dinner, I mean.

MARTIN

Make enough for three. If she doesn't appear, there'll be leftovers.

VERA

That seems—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Sad.

MARTIN

Why?

VERA

Setting a place and then—

MARTIN

Then what?

VERA

Clearing it away. Uneaten.

*MARTIN adjusts his watch, though it's already precise.*

MARTIN

You're catastrophizing.

VERA

I'm imagining.

MARTIN

It's the same thing.

VERA

No.

MARTIN

Yes. You imagine disasters, and then you live inside them.

VERA

I'm only thinking—

MARTIN

Too much.

VERA

That she might need—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Something.

MARTIN

Such as?

VERA

I don't know. That's the—I don't know what she needs.

MARTIN

Neither does she, probably.

*VERA looks at him. He doesn't return the look.*

VERA

Should we ask her?

MARTIN

Ask her what she needs?

VERA

Yes.

MARTIN

No.

VERA

Why not?

MARTIN

Because then she'd have to know. And she doesn't.

*Pause.*

Nobody knows what they need when they first arrive.

VERA

Arrive from where?

MARTIN

Wherever she was.

VERA

But where was—

MARTIN

Does it matter?

VERA

I thought it might. For context.

MARTIN

She's here now. That's the relevant information.

*VERA smooths the magazine page. It refuses to stay smooth.*

VERA

How long do you think before—

MARTIN

Before what?

VERA

Before she's ready to—

MARTIN

To what?

VERA

Talk about it.

*MARTIN finally looks at VERA directly. His face is careful.*

MARTIN

About what?

VERA

Whatever—

*Pause.*

Whatever it is.

MARTIN

There might not be an 'it.'

VERA

Martin.

MARTIN

What?

VERA

People don't just—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Appear.

MARTIN

She didn't appear. She arrived.

VERA

At four in the morning.

MARTIN

The train schedule isn't her fault.

VERA

With one bag.

MARTIN

Some people travel light.

VERA

And no warning.

MARTIN

She called.

VERA

Two hours before.

MARTIN

That's warning.

VERA

That's announcement.

*Pause.*

There's a difference.

*MARTIN moves toward the glass wall, looks out at the overgrown garden.*

MARTIN

The roses need cutting back.

VERA

I know.

MARTIN

They're going to seed.

VERA

Yes.

MARTIN

They'll strangle everything if we're not careful.

VERA

I'll do it tomorrow.

MARTIN

They should have been done last month.

VERA

I forgot.

MARTIN

How do you forget?

VERA

I've been—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Distracted.

MARTIN

By what?

*VERA doesn't answer. The fan rotates.*

I could do it.

VERA

The roses?

MARTIN

Yes.

VERA

You don't like gardening.

MARTIN

I don't mind cutting things back.

VERA

That's different from gardening.

MARTIN

Is it?

VERA

Gardening is helping things grow. What you're describing is—

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Pruning.

MARTIN

Same thing.

VERA

No.

*Pause.*

One is encouragement. The other is control.

*MARTIN turns from the window.*

MARTIN

You think I'm controlling.

VERA

I didn't say—

MARTIN

You implied it.

VERA

I was talking about roses.

MARTIN

Were you?

*Silence. Long. The quality of the light shifts almost imperceptibly.*

VERA

I should start the potatoes.

*She doesn't move.*

MARTIN

Yes.

*Neither of them moves.*

VERA

The fingerlings.

MARTIN

Right.

VERA

They take longer.

MARTIN

You mentioned that.

VERA

Did I?

MARTIN

Just now. About the potatoes.

VERA

I meant you mentioned it. Earlier.

MARTIN

Did I?

VERA

About them taking longer. About people underestimating—

MARTIN

Root vegetables.

VERA

Yes.

*She stands. Sits again.*

Maybe I'll wait.

MARTIN

For what?

VERA

To see if she—

MARTIN

She won't come down yet.

VERA

How do you know?

MARTIN

I don't. But you're acting as if—

VERA

What?

MARTIN

As if her coming down depends on you waiting.

VERA

That's not—

MARTIN

It is.

*Pause.*

You do this.

VERA

Do what?

MARTIN

Hold yourself hostage to other people's timing.

*VERA stands again. This time she stays standing.*

VERA

I'm going to check on her.

MARTIN

No.

VERA

Why not?

MARTIN

Because she's—

VERA

What?

MARTIN

Adjusting.

VERA

To what?

MARTIN

Being here.

VERA

She's been here for—

MARTIN

Seven hours.

VERA

You've been counting.

MARTIN

I looked at the clock when she arrived. I looked at it just now. That's mathematics, not counting.

VERA

There's a difference?

MARTIN

Yes.

*Pause.*

Counting implies vigilance. Mathematics is observation.

VERA

And you're just observing.

MARTIN

Yes.

VERA

Not vigilant.

MARTIN

No.

*VERA moves toward the door to the house. Stops.*

VERA

What if something's wrong?

MARTIN

Such as?

VERA

I don't know. That's why I want to check.

MARTIN

You checked earlier.

VERA

That was hours ago.

MARTIN

Three hours.

VERA

Still.

MARTIN

Three hours is nothing.

VERA

It is if—

MARTIN

If what?

*VERA's hand is on the door handle.*

VERA

If she's—

*She can't finish.*

MARTIN

She's fine.

VERA

How do you know?

MARTIN

Because if she weren't, we'd know.

VERA

That doesn't make sense.

MARTIN

Yes it does.

VERA

How?

MARTIN

We'd hear something. Or we wouldn't. Either way, we'd know.

*VERA removes her hand from the door.*

VERA

That's a terrible logic.

MARTIN

But it's logic.

VERA

It's absence pretending to be logic.

*MARTIN goes very still.*

MARTIN

I'm going to check something.

VERA

What?

MARTIN

In the house.

VERA

What thing?

MARTIN

Just—something.

*He moves toward the door. VERA steps aside.*

VERA

Martin.

MARTIN

What?

VERA

Are you checking on her?

MARTIN

I'm checking something.

VERA

That's not an answer.

MARTIN

It's the answer you're getting.

*He exits through the door to the house. VERA stands alone in the conservatory. She looks at the dead plant. At the water pooled in its saucer. At the garden beyond the glass. She sits in the wicker chair. The magazine is still open to the wrong page. The fan continues its slow rotation. She looks toward the door where MARTIN exited. Toward the ceiling. Toward the garden again.*

*She opens her mouth to speak. To whom? To the room? To herself? To the Claire who is somewhere above, facing a wall?*

*No sound comes out.*

*She closes her mouth.*

*The light shifts again. Or perhaps it was always this way, and we're only now noticing.*

*Outside, a rose petal falls.*

*The fan rotates.*

FADE TO:

---

INT. CONSERVATORY - EVENING

*The light has changed. Not darker, but thicker. The quality of late September, perhaps, or the memory of it. The overgrown garden beyond the glass walls is losing its edges to dusk.*

*CLAIRE (48) sits in the wicker chair, very still. A wine glass in her hand, half-empty. She hasn't turned on any lights. The ceiling fan turns.*

*The wooden door opens. HUGH (52) enters. He doesn't reach for the light switch. Doesn't greet her. Doesn't apologize for entering. He moves to the other chair, sits.*

*Silence.*

*The fan turns.*

*Claire takes a sip. Doesn't look at him.*

*Long pause.*

HUGH

You're like me.

*Claire's hand tightens on the glass. Very slightly.*

CLAIRE

I'm nothing like you.

HUGH

Exactly like me.

*Pause.*

CLAIRE

Is that why you came in here? To tell me that?

HUGH

I came in here because the room got quiet after you left.

CLAIRE

Quiet's not so terrible.

HUGH

Vera thinks it is.

*Claire sets down her glass. The water rings on the table accept it like an old habit.*

CLAIRE

Vera thinks a lot of things.

*Pause.*

You don't know me.

HUGH

I know you take up very little space. I know you apologize for existing. I know when Vera introduced us you looked at me like I was going to find you out.

CLAIRE

Find what out?

HUGH

Whatever you think needs finding.

*Silence.*

*The garden darkens. Or seems to. The light plays tricks here.*

CLAIRE

I work in data entry. Have for nine years. Same desk. Same—

*Pause.*

This is boring. You don't need to hear this.

*But continues anyway, compulsively.*

Same lunch at twelve-fifteen because everyone else goes at twelve-thirty. I dated someone for three years who I'm not sure—

*Stops.*

Actually, that's not fair to him. I probably did love him. I'm probably just saying I didn't because that sounds more—

*Pause.*

I ended it because I thought he deserved better. Which is what you say when you mean you're the problem but don't want to explain why.

HUGH

You're performing right now.

CLAIRE

What?

HUGH

This little speech. 'Same desk, same screen.' You've practiced it. Probably in this chair. Probably with that exact glass of wine.

*Pause.*

The boyfriend didn't leave because he realized nothing was there. He left because you kept telling him nothing was there. You made him believe it.

CLAIRE

You don't—

HUGH

I do. Because I've done it. But I don't dress it up as mercy.

*Claire looks at him for the first time. Really looks.*

*Silence.*

CLAIRE

You think you're saving her.

HUGH

I think we're talking about you.

CLAIRE

You think you're saving her. Vera. With your questions.

*Hugh pours more wine. First into Claire's glass, then his own. From a bottle we haven't seen until now.*

HUGH

I think I'm watching someone who needs to be watched.

CLAIRE

You think you're the only one who sees her.

HUGH

No.

*Pause.*

I think you're the other one. And I think that terrifies you.

*Claire stands. Moves to the glass wall. Looks at the garden, or at her reflection, or at nothing.*

*Long pause.*

CLAIRE

You think you're saving her. With your presence.

HUGH

No.

*Pause.*

I think I'm the guest who overstays. Who knows he overstays. Who stays anyway because the invitation keeps coming and at some point you stop asking why.

CLAIRE

I think I'm drowning and she's the person standing closest to the water.

*Hugh doesn't respond. Doesn't move. The statement sits between them like a third person.*

*Silence.*

*Claire turns from the glass.*

CLAIRE

I ruin things.

HUGH

I know.

*Pause.*

So do I.

CLAIRE

Then why are you here?

HUGH

I was invited.

CLAIRE

That's not an answer.

HUGH

Yes it is.

*Claire moves back to her chair. Sits. Picks up the wine glass.*

*Pause.*

CLAIRE

You came in here because the room got quiet. Because Vera notices quiet. Because when Vera notices things she asks about them and you'd rather—what? Control the conversation?

HUGH

I came in here because you left.

CLAIRE

I left because you were there.

HUGH

I know.

*Silence.*

*The ceiling fan turns. The room is almost dark now. Neither of them moves to turn on a light.*

CLAIRE

When did you realize you were like this?

HUGH

Like what?

CLAIRE

Exact. Mean. True.

*Hugh considers this.*

HUGH

I don't think I'm mean.

CLAIRE

No?

HUGH

I think I'm accurate.

*Pause.*

Mean would be lying to you. Telling you you're fine. That the person you dated definitely loved you and you probably loved him and everything's just—what? Nerves? Telling you Vera needs your presence the way you need hers. That it's balanced. That it's healthy.

CLAIRE

You don't know that it isn't.

HUGH

You just told me you're drowning.

*Claire drinks. Sets down the glass.*

CLAIRE

Figures of speech.

HUGH

Are very rarely figures.

*Long pause.*

*The door to the unseen house remains closed. Vera hasn't come looking. Or hasn't yet. Or won't.*

CLAIRE

What do you want from her?

HUGH

From Vera?

CLAIRE

What do you want?

HUGH

To be here. To be invited. To be the person who gets asked back.

*Pause.*

What do you want?

*Claire looks at him. The light—what's left of it—makes her face difficult to read.*

CLAIRE

To be needed.

HUGH

Not the same as being wanted.

CLAIRE

No.

*Pause.*

It's more reliable.

*Hugh almost smiles. Doesn't.*

HUGH

See? Like me.

*Claire doesn't answer. The fan turns. The garden is gone now, swallowed by evening. The glass walls reflect only the two of them, sitting in near-darkness, not looking at each other.*

*Silence.*

CLAIRE

She'll come looking.

HUGH

Eventually.

CLAIRE

She'll ask what we talked about.

HUGH

Yes.

CLAIRE

What will you tell her?

*Hugh stands. Moves toward the door. Stops.*

HUGH

That we sat quietly.

*Pause.*

That it was nice.

*He opens the door. Light spills in from the unseen house—warm, yellow, ordinary. He doesn't exit immediately.*

You think you're protecting her. From me.

CLAIRE

Aren't I?

HUGH

You're protecting her from you.

*Pause.*

I'm just easier to watch.

*He exits. The door closes. The warm light disappears.*

*Claire sits alone in the conservatory. The wine glass in her hand. The ceiling fan turning. She doesn't move to turn on a light.*

*Long pause.*

*She lifts the glass. Drinks. Sets it down on the water-ringed table, where it belongs.*

*The fan turns.*

*Silence.*

FADE TO:

INT. CONSERVATORY - AFTERNOON

*Afternoon light, but which afternoon? Late summer bleeding into autumn, or the memory of either. The conservatory attached to the unseen house. Glass walls on three sides reveal the overgrown garden—roses gone to seed, stone bench half-swallowed by ivy.*

*Inside: wicker furniture with faded cushions. A low table bearing water rings like archaeological evidence. The wooden door stage left leads to rooms we'll never enter.*

*The ceiling fan turns. Slowly. We hear nothing.*

*MARTIN sits in the best chair, reading. A hardcover book, spine cracked from proper use. He is fifty-something, impeccably dressed for nowhere. His posture suggests a man who has won an argument with gravity.*

*VERA occupies the loveseat, mending something—a pillowcase, perhaps. Her hands move with the automaticity of decades. She is Martin's age, or older, or younger. Time has made her uncertain.*

*CLAIRE stands at the glass wall, looking out. Thirty-something. Takes up remarkably little space for a person. She wears yesterday's clothes, or tomorrow's. One hand touches the glass, leaving no print.*

*No one speaks.*

*The fan turns.*

*Martin turns a page. The sound is very loud.*

*Vera's needle moves through fabric. Pull. Through. Pull. Through.*

*Claire's hand drops from the glass.*

CLAIRE

I'll leave tomorrow.

*Vera's hands stop. The needle suspended mid-air. She doesn't look at Claire.*

*Martin doesn't look up from his book.*

MARTIN

There's no need to rush.

*He turns another page.*

*Pause.*

CLAIRE

I make you uncomfortable.

MARTIN

Not at all.

*He continues reading. Or appearing to.*

CLAIRE

I make everyone uncomfortable.

*Pause.*

It's the only thing I'm good at.

*Vera sets down her mending carefully. As if it might break.*

VERA

Claire—

CLAIRE

Don't.

*She turns from the window. Looks at Vera now.*

Don't be kind. I can't—

*She stops.*

*Martin closes the book. Deliberately. The sound is very careful. He looks at Claire now. His eyes find her with the precision of a weapon.*

MARTIN

You've constructed a rather elegant little tragedy, haven't you? Complete with metaphor.

*Pause.*

"Uncomfortable." As if your presence were a force of nature. As if choice had nothing to do with it.

*He sets the book on the table. On top of the water rings.*

CLAIRE

I wanted to see if anyone else had survived.

*Silence.*

*The fan turns. One rotation. Two.*

MARTIN

Survived what?

*Claire doesn't look at him. Looks at Vera.*

CLAIRE

This.

*Pause.*

Whatever this is.

*Pause.*

Being somebody's daughter. Somebody's sister.

*Long pause.*

Somebody's wife.

*Martin stands.*

MARTIN

You've made survival into theater. Relationships into natural disasters.

*Pause.*

That's absurd.

*Claire finally looks at him.*

CLAIRE

Yes.

*Silence.*

*Vera stands. Walks to the glass wall. Her back to both of them. She touches the pane where Claire touched it moments before.*

VERA

Maybe it isn't.

*Martin stares at his wife's back.*

MARTIN

Vera—

VERA

I've always known it wasn't.

*Still to the glass.*

*Pause.*

I just needed someone else to say it first.

*Claire watches Vera's back. Her hand rises, falls. Rises again.*

CLAIRE

Do you ever feel—

*She stops.*

VERA

What?

*Still to the glass.*

*Claire steps back from the moment.*

CLAIRE

Nothing. Forget it.

*Pause.*

I shouldn't have come. This was—

*She looks at the door.*

I'll pack now, actually. Not tomorrow.

VERA

Claire—

*Turning.*

CLAIRE

No.

*She moves toward the door. Not the house door. The garden door.*

MARTIN

This is ridiculous.

VERA

Is it?

MARTIN

People have obligations. Responsibilities. That's not surviving, that's—

VERA

What?

MARTIN

Living. That's living.

*Vera looks at him for the first time.*

VERA

Is it?

*Silence.*

*Martin's hand gestures at the room. The gesture includes everything. The garden. The fan. His wife. Claire. Himself.*

MARTIN

This is perfectly—

*He stops.*

VERA

Say it.

MARTIN

Normal.

*The word sits in the air like something dead.*

CLAIRE

Yes.

*Very quietly.*

*She opens the door. The garden smell enters—green rot, late roses, earth.*

MARTIN

Where are you going?

*Claire doesn't answer. Steps through. Into the overgrown garden. The door stays open behind her.*

*Martin and Vera stand very still.*

*Through the glass, we see Claire walking into the roses. Her figure becoming less distinct. Or the light is changing. Or we're losing the ability to see clearly.*

MARTIN

She'll come back.

*Vera doesn't respond.*

MARTIN

She always comes back.

*Vera walks to the open door. Stands in the threshold. One foot in the conservatory. One foot in the garden.*

VERA

Does she?

*Martin looks at his wife. At her back. At the garden beyond. At Claire's disappearing figure.*

MARTIN

Vera—

*But Vera is already stepping through. Into the green. Into the rot. Into the late roses.*

*Martin stands alone in the conservatory.*

*The fan turns.*

*He walks to the open door.*

*Stands where Vera stood.*

*The garden waits.*

MARTIN

This is—

*He stops.*

*Long pause.*

This is perfectly—

*He stops again.*

*Through the glass walls, we see Vera has stopped walking. She stands among the roses. Waiting. Or not waiting. Simply standing.*

*Claire is farther out. Nearly invisible now in the overgrown green.*

*Martin's hand touches the doorframe.*

MARTIN

Normal.

*Very quietly.*

*But he doesn't move.*

*The fan turns.*

*The light continues changing.*

*And Martin stands in the threshold—*

*One foot in.*

*One foot out.*

*Unable to choose.*

*Unable to look away.*

*Silence.*

FADE TO:

---

INT. CONSERVATORY - AFTERNOON

*The next afternoon. The same afternoon. All afternoons.*

*The conservatory breathes its thick air. Beyond the glass: roses unpruned, patience rewarded with chaos.*

*CLAIRE's overnight bag sits by the door to the house. She sits furthest from it.*

*HUGH moves among them with a tea service—proper china, the ceremony of it. He pours. Offers biscuits on a plate. No one takes them.*

*MARTIN occupies his chair like a throne. VERA perches on the edge of hers.*

*The fan turns.*

HUGH

Milk?

*Placing cup before Claire.*

CLAIRE

No.

HUGH

Sugar?

CLAIRE

No.

HUGH

Vera?

*Pouring for Vera.*

VERA

Oh—yes. Both. Please. If it's not—

HUGH

It isn't.

*Pause.*

VERA

Thank you.

*Hugh pours for Martin without asking. Black. He knows. He pours for himself.*

*They hold their cups. No one drinks.*

MARTIN

I've arranged a car for four o'clock.

*To Claire.*

CLAIRE

Thank you.

*She doesn't move toward her bag.*

*Silence.*

VERA

Your garden must be—I mean, with all the rain we've had—

*To Hugh.*

HUGH

The rain helps.

VERA

Does it?

HUGH

When things are newly planted.

VERA

Oh.

*Pause.*

Are you planting? Now?

HUGH

Roses.

VERA

How lovely.

HUGH

The same variety as those.

*He gestures toward the garden beyond the glass.*

VERA

Really?

HUGH

But cared for.

MARTIN

Roses do perfectly well wild.

*Hugh sets down his cup.*

HUGH

Do they?

MARTIN

Nature doesn't require our intervention.

HUGH

Nature doesn't require anything.

*Long pause.*

But we're not discussing nature.

*Silence. The fan turns.*

CLAIRE

Will you come with me?

*To Vera, sudden.*

*Everyone stops. Even the light seems to hold.*

VERA

Where?

CLAIRE

Does it matter?

*Vera's cup trembles. Tea surfaces shiver.*

*Long pause.*

VERA

I don't—

MARTIN

Don't be ridiculous.

*Not to Claire.*

*A small sound escapes Vera—not quite a laugh. Helpless.*

VERA

No.

*Pause.*

I won't come.

*Claire nods. She knew. Knows. Will always know.*

*She rises. Takes her bag. The weight of it—everything she owns or nothing that matters.*

*She moves to the door. Stops. Turns back.*

CLAIRE

The conservatory is beautiful, Martin.

MARTIN

—

CLAIRE

All this glass.

*Pause.*

But what are you conserving?

*She wavers—almost stays, almost apologizes.*

I didn't mean—

*But she's already moving. The door. The exit. Not letting herself take it back or make it right.*

*Pause.*

MARTIN

Everything that matters.

*Carefully.*

*But Claire is already through the door. It closes behind her.*

*In the distance: the sound of the front door. Opening. Closing.*

*The three remain.*

*Silence.*

*Martin's words hang in the empty air. No one to receive them.*

*Hugh rises.*

HUGH

I should go.

VERA

Stay.

*She doesn't look at him when she says it.*

*Martin says nothing.*

*Hugh remains standing. Then sits.*

*Long pause.*

*Vera reaches for the biscuit plate. Takes one. Sets it on her saucer. Breaks it carefully. Into smaller pieces. Smaller still. She doesn't eat. Just breaks. Looking at the door Claire exited through.*

*Martin looks at his hands—their architecture, their emptiness.*

*Hugh looks at Vera.*

HUGH

You said no very quickly.

*To Vera.*

*Vera's hands stop. A piece of biscuit between her fingers.*

*Pause.*

Did you mean it?

*Vera looks at Hugh. At Martin. At the crumbs on her saucer.*

*Long silence.*

*The fan turns.*

*Outside, the garden continues its patient work of reclamation. The roses grow toward or away from something. Does it matter?*

*The light doesn't change. This afternoon. Every afternoon.*

*Martin's cup sits untouched on the table, the surface still as a mirror, reflecting only ceiling, only glass.*

*Vera's fingers hold the broken biscuit.*

*Hugh waits.*

*Silence.*

*The fan turns.*

*The light holds.*

*Fade—not to darkness, but to the turning. The endless, gentle, terrible turning.*

END OF PLAY
